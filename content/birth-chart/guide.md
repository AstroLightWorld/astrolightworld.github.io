# How to Read a Birth Chart

A birth chart is easiest to understand when you separate **where something is** from **what an astrology tradition says it means**.

AstroLight follows this basic sequence:

1. Convert the birth date and time to a precise astronomical time.
2. Calculate planetary positions.
3. Convert those positions into zodiac coordinates.
4. Calculate the Ascendant and house cusps from location and time.
5. Identify planetary aspects and, in Vedic mode, sidereal signs and Nakshatras.
6. Interpret those calculated coordinates using traditional astrology.

The important idea is simple: **the calculation tells us where something was; the astrological tradition supplies a framework for interpreting that position.**

## The four building blocks

A useful way to begin reading a chart is to separate four questions:

- **Planet — what?** What planetary function or theme is being described?
- **Sign — how?** Through what sign, element and modality is that symbolism expressed?
- **House — where?** Which area of life is emphasized?
- **Aspect — how does it interact?** What angular relationship does the planet have with other planets?

This gives AstroLight’s basic reading formula:

`Planet + Sign + House + Aspects`

Do not try to interpret every part of the chart at once. Start with the major placements, then add detail.

## Start with the three anchors

### The Sun

The Sun’s zodiac sign is commonly used as the “Sun sign.” It is one part of the chart and is traditionally associated with **identity, vitality and purpose**.

The Sun is not the whole personality in a birth chart. Its sign, house and aspects add context to the traditional interpretation.

### The Moon

The Moon moves much faster than the Sun and is therefore more sensitive to birth time. Traditional astrology associates it with **emotional patterns, habits, instinct and nurturing**.

In Vedic astrology, the Moon’s sidereal position is used to determine the **Janma Nakshatra**.

### The Ascendant

The Ascendant, or rising sign, is the zodiac degree rising on the eastern horizon at the birth location and time.

Because Earth rotates continuously, the Ascendant changes throughout the day. Accurate birth time and location therefore matter, especially for house calculations.

[Learn more about the rising sign →](../../learn/rising-sign/)

## Signs: the style of expression

The zodiac is divided into twelve 30° sectors. A planet’s sign describes the style through which its symbolism is traditionally expressed.

The twelve signs are grouped by:

- **Element:** Fire, Earth, Air and Water
- **Modality:** Cardinal, Fixed and Mutable

For example, a planet in a Fire sign is traditionally interpreted differently from the same planet in an Earth sign because the element and modality contribute a different style.

## How to read a planet in a house

The planet answers:

**“What kind of function or drive?”**

The house answers:

**“Where is that symbolism expressed?”**

For example, a planet placed in the 10th House would traditionally be read through themes such as career, reputation and public role. The planet’s sign would then describe the style of that expression, while aspects would add further context.

This is why a chart is more useful when placements are read together rather than as isolated keywords.

## How AstroLight calculates houses

AstroLight currently uses an **Equal House** model for the public birth-chart calculator. The Ascendant becomes the starting point of House 1, and each subsequent house begins exactly 30° later:

`House N cusp = Ascendant + (N − 1) × 30°`

This makes the method easy to inspect and explain. It is not the only house system used by astrologers; Placidus, Whole Sign, Koch and other systems can produce different cusps.

## Aspects: how planets interact

An aspect is an angular relationship between two planetary longitudes.

AstroLight checks common aspect families including:

| Aspect 					Approximate angle 					Traditional description 			 |      |                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Conjunction                                                    | 0°   | Planetary energies are closely combined       |
| Sextile                                                        | 60°  | An opportunity or cooperative relationship    |
| Square                                                         | 90°  | Tension, friction or a challenge to integrate |
| Trine                                                          | 120° | Ease, flow or supportive expression           |
| Opposition                                                     | 180° | Polarity, contrast or a need to balance       |

The exact separation and the chosen **orb** matter. AstroLight displays the angular relationship so the reader can see the numerical basis for the interpretation.

Aspects should not be read as isolated good/bad labels. They add another layer to the relationship between planetary placements.

## How to read a chart wheel

When AstroLight displays a chart wheel, start by locating:

1. **The Ascendant** — the rising point and beginning of House 1.
2. **The planets** — note each planet’s sign and house.
3. **The house cusps** — these show the boundaries of the twelve houses.
4. **The aspect relationships** — look for close angular relationships between planets.

A practical reading can then move from the large picture to the details:

`Sun / Moon / Ascendant → planets by sign → planets by house → major aspects`

You do not need to interpret every placement immediately.

## A simple example

Suppose a chart places **Venus in Taurus in the 7th House**.

A traditional reading can be built in layers:

- **Venus** supplies themes of relationships, attraction and values.
- **Taurus** supplies the sign style through which those themes are expressed.
- **7th House** supplies the traditional area of partnerships and one-to-one relationships.
- **Aspects** show how Venus is connected to other planetary positions.

The result is a layered interpretation rather than a single keyword.

This is the approach AstroLight aims to make visible: **calculation first, interpretation second.**

## Why birth time matters

Birth time affects the Ascendant and therefore the house framework.

The Ascendant changes continuously as Earth rotates. Because the house cusps are anchored to the Ascendant, an incorrect birth time can move planets from one house to another even when the planetary longitudes themselves remain nearly unchanged.

The Moon also moves relatively quickly, so birth time can matter when determining its exact position and, in Vedic mode, its Nakshatra and pada.

For the most precise result, use the best birth time and location available.

## Western / Tropical mode

AstroLight’s primary chart framework is **Western/Tropical astrology**.

The tropical zodiac divides the ecliptic into twelve 30° sectors beginning from the conventional tropical reference point. Planetary astronomical positions are mapped into those sectors to determine their tropical zodiac signs.

The calculated longitude is therefore kept separate from the traditional interpretation assigned to the sign.

[Learn about Sidereal vs Tropical →](../../learn/astrology-math/sidereal-vs-tropical/)

## Vedic / Sidereal mode

AstroLight also provides a Vedic mode using **sidereal longitude** rather than tropical longitude.

For this mode, AstroLight applies a **Lahiri-style ayanamsa approximation**, then maps the Moon’s sidereal longitude into one of **27 Nakshatras**, each divided into four **padas**.

This means the same astronomical planetary position can receive a different zodiac sign in Tropical and Sidereal frameworks because the two systems use different reference frames.

## How AstroLight calculates a chart

At a high level, the calculation pipeline is:

1. Read the supplied birth date and local time.
2. Apply the supplied UTC offset to establish the astronomical time.
3. Calculate planetary ecliptic longitudes.
4. Map longitudes into the chosen zodiac framework.
5. Calculate the Ascendant from birth time and location.
6. Calculate Equal House cusps from the Ascendant.
7. Determine planetary house placements.
8. Calculate angular relationships between planetary longitudes.
9. In Vedic mode, convert to sidereal longitude and calculate the Moon’s Nakshatra and pada.
10. Present the calculated positions alongside traditional interpretive explanations.

This separation is deliberate: AstroLight is designed to show the numerical/astronomical basis before applying the interpretive layer.

## A beginner’s five-question workflow

When you first see your chart, ask:

### 1. What is my Sun sign?

Start with the Sun’s sign and its traditional themes.

### 2. What is my Moon sign?

Then look at the Moon’s sign and house. In Vedic mode, note the Moon’s Nakshatra and pada as well.

### 3. What is my Ascendant?

The rising sign establishes the beginning of the Equal House framework used by AstroLight.

### 4. Where are the other planets?

For each major planet, note:

`Planet → Sign → House`

Then look at close aspects.

### 5. What patterns repeat?

Look for themes that appear in more than one place. A repeated theme across a planet, sign, house or aspect can provide a useful traditional interpretation without relying on a single placement.
